How your credit score is weighted

You have more than one credit score, and the one in your app may not be the one a lender pulls. Here's how the three most common models weigh your credit, side by side.

What each model weighs

These are the general weights FICO and VantageScore publish. They're a guide, not a formula: FICO says how much each category matters can vary from one person to another. FICO's mortgage, auto and card versions and FICO 10T use the same five categories, but FICO doesn't publish separate percentages for them.

Most of your score is a cash-flow question

The two biggest pieces of every model are paying on time and how much of your available credit you're using. Neither one is a credit trick. Both come down to knowing what's coming in, what's due and when, before the bill shows up.

A missed payment usually isn't about not caring. It's a due date that landed before payday, or a card that quietly carried this month's groceries. A budget puts those dates and balances where you can see them.

What changes besides the weights

The bigger differences between models are in what data they read and how far back they look.

A trend, not a snapshot

FICO 8 and VantageScore 3.0 look at your balances as of the latest report. VantageScore 4.0 looks back up to two years, and FICO 10T also uses trended data.

Paying a card down steadily shows up as a pattern, not a one-month blip.

Short credit histories

FICO needs an account at least six months old and some activity in the last six months. VantageScore can score you with a single account on file, even a new one.

Just starting out? You may have a VantageScore before you have a FICO.

Rent can count

The newer models can include rent payments when they're reported to the credit bureaus. Most rent isn't reported unless your landlord or a rent-reporting service sends it.

On-time rent only helps if it's on your report.

Mortgages are switching models

For decades, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans required Classic FICO. That's changing. Updated October 2026.

  1. October 2022

    The agency that oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac picks FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 as the newer scores for their loans.

  2. April 22, 2026

    Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac start accepting VantageScore 4.0 from approved lenders in a limited rollout. FHA says it will allow both newer models.

  3. September 2026

    Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac expand VantageScore 4.0 after being told to approve all lenders to use it.

  4. January 1, 2027

    FHA loans can use VantageScore 4.0 or FICO 10T alongside Classic FICO.

  5. Date not set

    FICO 10T for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans.

Until your lender switches, expect Classic FICO: one older FICO version from each of the three bureaus, with the middle score usually deciding your rate.
