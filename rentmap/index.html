3BR rent is the average of two figures for each metro. The first is the median gross rent for 3-bedroom units from the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey, 2020–2024 5-year estimates (table B25031), which is what current renters pay, including utilities. The second is HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for a 3-bedroom, effective October 1, 2026, which is closer to what someone moving in today would pay. Where a metro spans more than one HUD rent area, HUD values are weighted by each county's renter households. ACS tops out at $3,500, so San Jose's Census figure is $3,500+.

Median household income is from the same ACS 2020–2024 estimates (table B19013), in 2024 dollars. It counts every earner in the household. Income needed (30% rule) is the yearly pre-tax income at which this rent equals 30% of pay: monthly rent × 12 ÷ 0.30.

Minimum wage is the rate in effect October 2026 in each metro's principal (first-named) city: the city or county ordinance where one exists, otherwise the state rate, otherwise the federal $7.25. Sources: U.S. Department of Labor state table and EPI's Minimum Wage Tracker, both updated July 1, 2026; Florida's scheduled increase to $15.00 on September 30, 2026 is included. Other cities and suburbs in a metro can have different rates. Ohio's rate applies to businesses with $405K+ in sales, Montana's to $110K+, and West Virginia's to employers with 6+ workers. Min-wage hours is monthly rent divided by that hourly rate; a full-time month is about 173 hours.

Metro areas are the 393 Metropolitan Statistical Areas in OMB's July 2023 delineations, drawn from their counties. Rent and income are metro-wide figures and vary a lot by neighborhood.
